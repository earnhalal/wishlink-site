What Is a Digital Birthday Card? (And Why Everyone Is Sending Them)

Paper cards are lovely — and increasingly rare. Their digital grandchildren have taken over: interactive, personal, and delivered in one tap. Here is the honest comparison.

From paper to post to… page

Birthday greetings have moved three times: from paper cards, to wall posts and chat messages, to something new — a dedicated page made for one person. A chat message sits in a thread and scrolls away. A page is a place: it has their name on the door, a lock only they can open, and a whole little celebration inside.

What a digital birthday page can do that a card cannot

  • Lock with a PIN — a tiny ritual that makes opening it feel like unwrapping.
  • Play music and speak — the birthday tune, and their name said out loud.
  • Show your photos — sliding by like a little film of your favourite moments.
  • Be interactive — candles to blow out, a cake to cut, a gift box hiding your real message.
  • Arrive anywhere, instantly — one link on WhatsApp, no postage, no “the card is still in the mail”.

Digital vs paper: the honest table

  • Speed: paper takes days; a page takes two minutes to make and one second to arrive.
  • Distance: paper struggles across borders; a link does not know what a border is.
  • Personalisation: paper gives you a blank inside cover; a page gives you photos, music, a letter and a hidden gift.
  • Keepsake: paper wins on the shelf — but a link can be re-opened on every phone they ever own, and the page can also be downloaded as a file to keep.
  • Cost: a good paper card plus postage costs more than zero. A WishLink page is free.

When paper is still better

For a grandparent who treasures the mantelpiece row of cards, or a milestone birthday where the card itself is the keepsake — send paper, proudly. For everyone else, and especially for the people far away, the page wins on impact per minute spent.

The real answer most people land on: send the page at midnight, and if there is a paper card, let it arrive as the sequel.
